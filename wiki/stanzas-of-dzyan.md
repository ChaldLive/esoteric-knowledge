# Stanzas of Dzyan

The root-text from which both Blavatsky's *The Secret Doctrine* (1888) and Bailey's *A Treatise on Cosmic Fire* (1925) unfold. Thirteen stanzas are given at the opening of TCF; the body of the book is their extended commentary.

## Origin and Status

Blavatsky claimed the Stanzas are translated from the *Book of Dzyan*, a text written in Senzar (a sacred language predating Sanskrit), preserved in the archives of the adept hierarchy. Bailey's DK presents TCF as a further commentary on the same root material.

The stanzas cannot be verified by external scholarship — their status is taken on faith as received teaching, or engaged with as a coherent mythological cosmology.

## The Stanzas in TCF

TCF gives 13 stanzas. Their content traces:
1. The precosmic state before manifestation
2. The first stirrings of cosmic ideation
3. The differentiation of primordial matter
4. The emergence of the planes
5. The coming of the Builders
6. The lighting of the fires
7–13. The further unfolding of cosmos and the human kingdom

## Relationship to *The Secret Doctrine*

The Stanzas in *The Secret Doctrine* cover cosmogenesis and anthropogenesis in more detail. Bailey reads the same root text through the lens of [[the-three-fires]].

## Method of Commentary

Bailey/DK explicitly state that the full meaning of the Stanzas cannot be given publicly — each [[initiation]] unlocks further levels of understanding. Higher keys remain withheld.
