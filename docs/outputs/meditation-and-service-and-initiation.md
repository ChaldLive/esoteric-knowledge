# The Science of Meditation and the Science of Service, Correlated with Initiation

*Education in the New Age* names three sciences that will govern the education of the Aquarian Age:
the Science of the Antahkarana, the Science of Meditation, and the Science of Service. The
correlation of the first with initiation is already established in [[antahkarana]]. This note takes
up the other two, drawing on EITN's own definitions and on explicit statements found in
*Discipleship in the New Age*, Vols. I and II.

## The Science of Meditation

EITN is explicit about what Meditation *is*, structurally: not an independent science, but "a
subsidiary branch, preparatory to the Science of the Antahkarana... the true science of occult
bridge building." Specifically:

> "It is intended primarily to: a. Produce sensitivity to the higher impressions. b. Build the
> first half of the antahkarana, that between the personality and the soul... It might be added
> here that the building of the second half of the antahkarana (that which bridges the gap in
> consciousness between the soul and the spiritual triad) is called the science of vision, because
> just as the first half of the bridge is built through the use of mental substance, so the second
> half is built through the use of light substance." (EITN, Ch. III)

*Discipleship in the New Age* Vol. I confirms and dates this. Describing how the antahkarana's
personality-to-soul links are actually built, it names meditation as one of four concurrent
techniques:

> "Many...are in process of linking the three lower aspects, which we call the personality, with
> the soul itself, through meditation, discipline, service and directed attention... The process of
> establishing this interlinking and interrelation, and the strengthening of the bridge thus
> constructed, goes on until the third initiation." (DINA I)

**Correlation:** Meditation's building work is therefore bracketed exactly as [[antahkarana]]'s
own building-work is bracketed — beginning in preparation for the 2nd initiation and completing,
structurally, at the 3rd. This is a direct restatement, not a new correlation; EITN's "Science of
Meditation" is simply the technique-name for the antahkarana's first phase, and its unnamed
second phase ("the science of vision") for the antahkarana's second phase, which R&I elsewhere
completes at the 3rd–4th initiation (see [[antahkarana]]'s "Relationship to Initiation" table).

DINA Vol. II shows this technique continuing in practice among senior, already-accepted disciples
— the Tibetan assigns specific, ray-differentiated group meditations explicitly "in preparation
for initiation" (DINA II, "Teachings on Meditation," Section Two) — confirming Meditation is not a
beginner's technique that is later abandoned, but one that intensifies and specializes as the
disciple approaches the 3rd degree.

## The Science of Service

EITN frames Service very differently from Meditation — not as a building technique in its own
right, but as the **consequence** of the other two sciences succeeding:

> "The Science of Service grows normally and naturally out of the successful application of the
> other two sciences... Service is the true science of creation and is a scientific method of
> establishing continuity." (EITN, Ch. III)

*Discipleship in the New Age* Vol. II gives a far stronger and more specific statement — one that
inverts EITN's apparent ranking and ties Service directly to the *culminating* initiation of the
human-kingdom sequence, the 4th:

> "You will all awaken some day to the realisation that the Science of Service is of greater
> importance than the Science of Meditation, because it is the effort and the strenuous activity of
> the serving disciple which evokes the soul powers, makes meditation an essential requirement, and
> is the mode—ahead of all others—which invokes the Spiritual Triad, brings about the
> intensification of the spiritual life, forces the building of the antahkarana, and leads in a
> graded series of renunciations to the Great Renunciation, which sets the disciple free for all
> eternity." (DINA II, January 1946 instruction)

"The Great Renunciation" is Bailey's standing name for the **4th initiation** (Crucifixion /
Renunciation in [[initiation]]'s table — the destruction of the causal body). This is the single
most explicit initiation-correlation available for Service: not a technique confined to one stage,
but the sustained *driving force*, described as prior to and causative of antahkarana-building
itself, across the entire arc from accepted discipleship to the 4th initiation.

**Reconciling the two statements.** EITN (1954) presents Service as the downstream *result* of
Meditation and Antahkarana-work succeeding. DINA II (1946, so textually earlier though published
later) presents Service as the upstream *cause* that "forces" antahkarana-building and "makes
meditation an essential requirement." These are not strictly contradictory — read together, they
describe a feedback loop rather than a one-way sequence: early, effortful service (even before
any bridge exists) creates the karmic and magnetic pressure that necessitates meditation and
bridge-building; as the bridge matures, service becomes more effective and less effortful, which
in turn accelerates further building. Neither text states this reconciliation directly — it is
offered here as the most consistent reading, not a quoted synthesis.

### Service's Changing Character Across the Path

DINA Vol. I independently confirms that Service does not vanish or diminish as one advances — it
changes *character*, and this change tracks the [[discipleship]] ladder closely:

| Stage | Quality of Service |
|---|---|
| Aspirant / Probationer (pre-[[discipleship|Little Chelaship]]) | Effortful, self-conscious, mixed with "personal reasons for service"; part of the exoteric foundation expected of "all true aspirants" alongside purification and devotion |
| Accepted Discipleship (discipleship stage III) | Impersonal; directed by the needs of the Ashram rather than personal inclination; the disciple's "gauge of capacity lies in his influence...upon other people" |
| Chela within the Master's Heart and beyond (stage VI, post-4th initiation) | No longer an act of will at all — "his work is that of radiation—a radiation which evokes activity in others, initiates a response from his fellowmen" (DINA I) |

This progression — from effortful service, to impersonal service, to spontaneous radiation — is the
experiential correlate of the initiation ladder Service is said to drive. It is consistent with,
though not identical to, R&I's general principle that later initiations replace deliberate action
with spontaneous radiatory being.
